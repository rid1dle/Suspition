# Design: Hot seats (making the room the game)

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-10-18
Branch: feat/first-playable
Repo: rid1dle/Suspition
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Builder

## Problem Statement

In the creator's own test games with friends, Suspition feels like an info dump, not a party. The flat moments were:
- people confused about what is happening;
- "what is my character even";
- no interest in learning about their own or other characters;
- silent stretches where everyone figures things out alone on their phone.

The phone is where the game happens and the room is where people wait. Nobody has a reason to look up, talk, or look at the phone at the right moment.

## What Makes This Cool

The AI Game Master stops being an oracle you read and becomes a puppeteer that stages clashes between friends. The TV calls "HOT SEAT: PRIYA, 90 seconds". Each other phone gets one sharp question to ask her out loud. At the same moment Priya's phone whispers how to survive it: lie, dodge, or blame someone. You find out who your character is by being grilled in front of people who know your face. No human has to sit out to run it, which is what killed the group's human-GM mystery (it worked, but stuck only once in 4 tries).

## Constraints

- Played in one room by 3 to 8 friends, phones plus a shared table screen. It must work at 3 players.
- The group loves Secret Hitler and Among Us and plays Catan a lot. Deduction should stay real; the mystery is still the point.
- A fresh AI-written world every game stays; hand-written scenarios are not the answer.
- The reader wall stays: player text never steers the GM. Whispers are written by the GM, never by players.
- Plain simple English everywhere. Readability is never traded for style.
- GM calls cost the host's Claude usage, so the beats for a round should be written in as few calls as possible.

## Premises

1. **The phone is a prompter.** The room is the game; the phone mostly hands you something to do or say out loud, not things to read.
2. **Private GM chat is scarce and purposeful.** Each private answer gives you something to use in the room: a clue, a secret, a lead. Reading per turn stays under about 20 seconds.
3. **Pace comes from a few loud moments and spotlight turns.** A few loud shared moments plus spotlight turns mean someone is always "up" and there is something fresh to argue about. Nobody can sit out; quiet players get small, easy actions.
4. **Identity starts as a playable hook.** One line, one secret, one person you distrust. The rest unlocks during play.
5. **Pointing is free and loud; the formal vote never removes anyone or ends the game** (revised in this session). Its stakes are information and suspicion: the most-voted player has one line of their secret made public, and a wrong group pick gives the culprit a little cover. The real verdict stays at the end. This works from 3 players up. The creator rejected a costly arrest: "you try arresting one he is not then game over" in a 3-player game.

## Cross-Model Perspective

A fresh Claude subagent (Codex not installed) read a summary of the session:
- **Coolest version:** "the GM briefs both sides of the same scene". It hands the asker a question and the seated player a whisper on how to lie, dodge or blame, then tells the asker when the answer was a lie.
- **What excites the creator:** spotlight turns, "the only answer with no hedge". It argued the deduction should be the backdrop. We kept deduction real instead: the group plays Catan and wanted to solve the last mystery.
- **Challenged premise 5:** pointing should be free, because "sus!" is free and constant in Among Us. That led to the revision above.
- **Closest existing game:** Blood on the Clocktower, which already has a storyteller, useful whispers, free talk and public nominations. Suspition's part is an AI storyteller (nobody sits out), timing whispers into clashes, and a fresh story each game.
- **Its first build:** a minute-by-minute 10-minute round:
  1. a loud event
  2. hot seat
  3. free talk with Point
  4. hot seat
  5. a private beat
  6. a vote that reveals a secret line
  7. a cliffhanger
- **Its success measures:** no silence over 60 seconds, everyone speaks 3 or more times per round, someone asks for "one more round".

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Hot seats in the current rounds (chosen)
Add hot seats with both-sides whispers, a free Point button, hook-first cards and two-line private answers to today's round structure. Medium effort, low risk. It reuses moments, events, the round vote and the reader wall.

### Approach B: Storyteller beat clock (the destination)
Every round runs on a fixed, server-driven beat clock:
1. a loud event
2. hot seat 1
3. free talk, phones dark except Point
4. hot seat 2
5. a private beat
6. the spotlight vote
7. a cliffhanger

Large effort. It is deferred until hot seats prove themselves at a real game night.

### Approach C: Interrogation duels
Paired scenes with clashing briefs, played like a short play, and the room votes on who lied. Not chosen: it is slow for big groups, heavy for shy players, and drifts away from the quick back-and-forth the group loves.

## Recommended Approach

Approach A now, built so it grows into B.

1. **Hot seat** (the core).
   - Players take turns being "up" in a fair rotation (see How it plays for how many seats a round). The table screen shows the name, a 90-second timer and a sound.
   - **Questions:** up to 3 other players' phones each show one question to ask out loud. The question is written for that asker from what their character could know, and points at something real in the case.
   - **Whisper:** the seated player's phone shows how to handle it from their card's truth: what is safe to admit, what to lie about, and who to deflect onto. A culprit gets a believable lie; a detective gets a choice to protect a secret or come clean.
   - **Follow-up:** an asker whose card contradicts a likely lie may get one conditional follow-up line (see How it plays).
   - **One GM call** writes the round's hot seats when the round starts (questions, whispers and follow-ups), so nobody waits mid-round.
   - **The wall:** whispers and questions come from the GM's sheets only. Player text never reaches them, so the reader wall is unchanged.
2. **Point** (free, any time).
   - One tap on a player's name: "Sam suspects Priya!" shows on the table with a 20-second "defend yourself" timer.
   - There is no cost.
   - A short cooldown per player (for example 60 seconds) stops spam. To be tuned in play.
3. **Spotlight vote.**
   - In Hot seats style it replaces today's round question: "Who goes in the cellar?"
   - Votes are secret, then revealed together on the table.
   - The most-voted player has one line of their secret made public. Nobody is removed and the game never ends early.
   - If the vote lands on someone who is not a culprit, the culprit gets a little cover (see How it plays).
4. **Hook-first card.**
   - The Card tab opens on three things: who you are in one line, one secret to protect, and one person you distrust and why.
   - Your side, alibi and real night timeline always stay visible. Wants, fears and reasons unlock during play (see How it plays). Each unlock is a small alert.
5. **Two-line private answers.**
   - Private GM replies are capped at two short lines and end with something to do or say in the room ("Show Raj the key", "Ask Lata out loud why she lied").
   - Fewer private questions per round, to make each one count.
6. **Loud, few moments.**
   - Round events take over the table screen with a sound and a short read-aloud line. At most one or two per round.
   - No drip of small news.

Grow into B: once hot seats work, the round clock (event, seat, talk, seat, private beat, vote, cliffhanger) becomes the server's beat schedule.

## How it plays (build decisions)

These are my calls, made so the first build is concrete. Each one is open to the creator's change.

**Round style.**
- A room setting: "Hot seats" (new, the default for game night) or "Classic". Classic is today's per-round multiple-choice question where a wrong answer replays the round.
- In Hot seats style, the spotlight vote replaces that question. Each round's reward still appears at round end, as the cliffhanger line.

**Who is up, and how often.**
- A fair rotation queue, ordered by the GM at game start.
- Seats per round = players ÷ rounds, rounded up: at least 1, at most 3. So everyone sits at least once (3 rounds and 8 players gives 3 seats a round).

**What starts a seat.**
- Seat 1 starts 60 seconds after the round's loud event.
- Later seats are spaced evenly through the round timer.
- The host can start the next seat early with "Hot seat now".
- If a round ends before a seat runs, that player goes first next round.

**Reading time.**
- The seated player's phone says "You're up in 15 seconds" and shows the whisper first, the same get-ready step moments use.
- The whisper is at most 3 lines of at most 12 words each.
- The 90-second timer starts after that.

**Who asks.** At most 3 askers per seat, rotating so every player asks during the round. Each asker gets one question of at most 15 words.

**Follow-ups.**
- The server never hears spoken answers, so follow-ups are conditional lines the asker reads only if they fit: "If Priya says she was in the library, tell her: the library was locked at nine."
- An asker gets one only when their own card truly contradicts a likely lie.
- No path needs a tap or any typed text, so the reader wall is untouched.

**Clue pacing.**
- The round-start call gets the round's allowed facts.
- Seats, follow-ups and reveals in early rounds may only touch side secrets: a player's private secret that is not the crime.
- Facts that point at the culprit or method unlock from the second-to-last round, in step with today's clue reveal rounds.
- The bot playtest judge flags any game "solved before the final vote".

**Spotlight vote.**
- No self-votes. Votes are secret, then revealed together.
- A tie, including a 3-player 1-1-1 split, gives no reveal: the table says "The room is split."
- The most-voted player's next reveal line goes public. The world schema pre-writes 3 ordered reveal lines per player: true, short, and never naming the culprit, the method or the motive. When a player's list runs out, the table says "Nothing new about Priya."

**Cover.**
- Only the spotlight vote gives cover. When the most-voted player is not a culprit, suspicion drops by 10 on the existing meter, and each culprit's next whisper says the room is looking elsewhere.
- Point never changes anything; it stays free.

**Point.**
- It cannot be used during a hot seat; the seat is already the spotlight.
- One Point banner at a time on the table, at most 2 waiting, extras dropped. A 60-second cooldown per player.

**Table screen priority.** Loud event, then vote reveal, then hot seat, then Point. A lower item waits for a higher one.

**The round-start GM call.**
- It runs in the background after the round starts, and the 60-second lead-in hides its time.
- A 60-second time limit, one retry, and output checked against the schema.
- **Fallback:**
  - Seats still run, with plain questions built from the seated player's told alibi ("Where were you at 9:30?").
  - The whisper is that player's own hook line.
  - No follow-ups.

**Card unlocks.**
- **Always shown:** your side (a culprit knows it is the culprit), your hook, what you told people (alibi), and your real night timeline. Hot seats need these.
- **Locked at first:** "What drives you" (wants, fears) unlocks after your first hot seat. "Why you did what you did" unlocks after your second seat, or when you top a spotlight vote.
- Hidden facts unlock as today, when the GM reveals them.
- This is server logic: per-player `unlocked` state, not a GM field.
- Questions, whispers and follow-ups may only use always-shown, unlocked or public facts.

## Open Questions

- **The replay quiz.** Should the per-round multiple-choice question (wrong answers replay the round) be cut, kept, or merged into the spotlight vote? The reviewer called it "homework"; test before deciding.
- **The Point cooldown.** How long it is, and whether Point shows on every phone or only on the table.
- **Choosing who is up.** Does the GM choose (for drama) or does it rotate fairly? The likely answer is a mix: rotate, with the GM choosing the order.
- **Shy players.** Should a seated player be able to pass the seat once per game (using an override)?
- **Moments.** Should today's moments (scripted lines) fold into hot-seat follow-ups, or stay separate?

## Success Criteria

Measured at the next real game night with the same friends:
- No silence longer than 60 seconds during a round.
- Every player speaks out loud at least 3 times per round.
- Nobody asks "what is my character?" after the first hot seat.
- Someone asks for "one more round" or another game.
- Players read their phone in short glances, not long reads. Private replies stay at two lines.

## Distribution Plan

Existing setup covers it: the host runs `npm start` on a laptop on the same Wi-Fi, and phones join by QR. No new distribution.

## Next Steps

1. **Hot seat engine.**
   - A new server state for the seated player and timer, and one GM call at round start that writes two seats (questions per asker, whisper, follow-up).
   - Table banner and timer, phone question card, phone whisper card.
2. **Point.** The button on each phone, the table banner, a 20-second defense timer and a cooldown.
3. **Hook-first card.** The GM world schema adds `hook`, `distrusts` and `distrust_why` per player. The card opens on these, and the other sections lock until they're unlocked.
4. **Two-line private answers.** A prompt rule plus a schema cap; each answer ends with a room action.
5. **Spotlight vote** with a secret-line reveal, using the round vote and suspicion meter.
6. **Playtest.**
   - Bots first: add a hot-seat mode to the harness.
   - Then the real game night. Measure the success criteria by watching the room, not the logs.

## What I noticed about how you think

- You judged ideas by the room, not the feature. "should be loud to garner everyones attention and not just every 2-3 mins" puts attention first and frequency second.
- You defended a premise with a concrete case: "you try arresting one he is not then game over" with 3 players. That changed the design; the vote now reveals rather than removes.
- You were honest about your own uncertainty ("I could be wrong here too") on one-word identity, which led to the hook-first middle path instead of either extreme.

Findings below are retained from the last completed review; the current document remains unreviewed.

### R1-1 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> The spotlight vote makes 'one line of their secret' public for the most-voted player, but the document never says what that line may contain. If the culprit is most-voted, a raw secret line can give away the solution and end the mystery early, which breaks Premise 5 ('the real verdict stays at the end') and the constraint that deduction stays real. It also does not say what happens when a player who is voted top again has no secret lines left.

**Remedy**

> Say that the GM world schema pre-writes a short ordered list of safe reveal lines per player (true but never solution-revealing for the culprit). Say what the reveal shows once a player's list runs out (for example, the next-most-voted player's line is shown, or a generic 'nothing new' line).

### R1-2 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The spotlight vote has no tie rule and no self-vote rule. At 3 players a 1-1-1 split is likely, so 'the most-voted player' often does not exist in exactly the player count the design must support.

**Remedy**

> Decide whether self-votes are allowed, and set a tie rule (for example: no reveal, every tied player reveals, or the GM breaks the tie from suspicion). Check that it works at 3 players.

### R1-3 — clarity (blocking)

**Problem**

> 'If the group pointed at the wrong person, the culprit gets a little cover' does not say whether 'pointed' means Point taps or the spotlight vote. It also does not say what 'cover' changes in the suspicion meter, or how the player or GM sees that change. Point is described as 'free' with 'no cost', so if Point taps feed cover, that contradicts the free framing.

**Remedy**

> Name the trigger (the vote only, or Point too), the exact suspicion-meter effect, and where that effect shows up (GM prompts, culprit whisper, table). If Point feeds it, take back the 'no cost' claim.

### R1-4 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> The follow-up fires when 'a player whose knowledge contradicts the answer' is found. But the answer is spoken out loud, and the server never hears it. The follow-up is also pre-written at round start, before anyone answers. A detective who chose to 'come clean', or a culprit who ignored the whisper, would get a wrong 'That's not true' follow-up. The game cannot decide on its own whether the answer was a lie.

**Remedy**

> Say what triggers the follow-up. For example, the seated player taps the path they took (admit / lie / dodge) from a fixed menu, which is not free text, so the reader wall holds. Or the follow-up is a conditional line the asker reads only if the answer matched a stated claim. Then say what the asker sees for each path.

### R1-5 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> Hot-seat questions 'point at something real in the case', the culprit gets lie guidance, and follow-ups expose contradictions. But there is no rule on how much each seat, follow-up or vote reveal may tell. A round-1 follow-up that catches the culprit's lie could solve the mystery long before the end verdict, which goes against the 'deduction should stay real' constraint.

**Remedy**

> Add a clue-pacing rule to the round-start GM call: what each round's seats and follow-ups may reveal (for example, early follow-ups expose side secrets only, and lies that point at the culprit unlock in later rounds), and check it in the bot harness.

### R1-6 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> Approach A adds hot seats to 'today's round structure' without the beat clock (which is put off to B). Nothing says what starts each of the two seats inside a round: a host tap, a fixed time after round start, after an event, or something else. That leaves the core mechanic with no start trigger.

**Remedy**

> Say how and when each hot seat starts in Approach A (who or what triggers it, and where it sits relative to events, private beats and the vote), and what happens if a round ends before the second seat runs.

### R1-7 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> The design commits to 'spread so everyone gets a turn across the game' with two seats per round. With 8 players that needs at least 4 rounds, and the document never states the game length. The selection rule is also both committed ('the GM picks') and left as an Open Question ('GM chooses or rotates'), so nothing guarantees the coverage it promises.

**Remedy**

> Pick the selection rule for the build (for example, a fair rotation where the GM orders the queue) and make sure everyone gets a seat for the real round count, or change the promise (for example, seats per round grow with player count).

### R1-8 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> Every other player gets one question to ask inside a fixed 90-second seat. At 8 players that is 7 questions plus answers, about 13 seconds each, with no time left for follow-ups or argument. The success criteria and the 'quick back-and-forth' goal depend on this timing working.

**Remedy**

> Scale the timer with the number of askers, or cap the askers per seat (for example 3, rotating so each player asks across the round), and say which.

### R1-9 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The whole round depends on one big GM call at round start (questions for each asker, whispers and follow-ups for two seats). There is no behavior for a slow call, a failed call, or output that breaks the schema. If it fails there is no hot seat at all, and round start may stall while the call runs.

**Remedy**

> State a time limit and retry, a validation step against the schema, and a fallback (for example, start the round and run seats once ready, or a simpler seat with generic questions from the GM's sheets), plus what the table shows while waiting.

### R1-10 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> The hook-first card locks the night timeline, wants and fears until they unlock during play. But hot seats ask players about the case, and follow-ups rely on 'a player whose knowledge contradicts the answer'. A seated or asking player may need timeline knowledge their card has not shown them yet. The document also does not say whether the culprit's hook tells them they are the culprit.

**Remedy**

> Say what each player must always see (for example, their own alibi or timeline facts any question or follow-up could touch, and whether they are the culprit), and keep the questions, whispers and follow-ups in the GM's output limited to unlocked or always-shown facts.

### R1-11 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The table screen now has to show hot-seat banners and a 90-second timer, Point banners with 20-second defense timers (up to 8 players, each with only a 60-second cooldown), full-screen round events, and vote reveals. Point is allowed 'any time', but there is no rule for what wins when these overlap, or whether Point works during a hot seat.

**Remedy**

> Set a table-screen priority and queue rule (for example, events and vote reveals come before hot seats, which come before Point; Point is held back or queued during seats; one Point banner at a time).

### R1-12 — clarity (blocking)

**Problem**

> The whisper (what to admit, what to lie about, who to blame) arrives 'at the same moment' the 90-second seat starts, so the seated player must read it while being questioned. Premise 2 limits reading to about 20 seconds, but the whisper has no length cap and no read-in time.

**Remedy**

> Cap the whisper length (for example, three short lines) and decide whether there is a short read-in before the timer starts, or whether the whisper arrives before the seat is announced.

### R1-13 — clarity (blocking)

**Problem**

The spotlight vote 'replaces or sits next to' it, while the Open Question asks whether to cut, keep or merge the quiz. Next Steps builds the spotlight vote 'using the round vote', so the build target is unclear.

**Remedy**

### R1-14 — clarity (blocking)

Next Steps adds only `hook`, `distrusts` and `distrust_why` to the schema, with no unlock state or unlock order.

**Remedy**
